Executive Summary

The Crisis of Access

In Ethiopia, menstruation is more than a biological process—it is a barrier to opportunity. Over 70% of women and girls lack access to the products, facilities, and information needed to manage their periods with dignity.

This crisis is fueled by a period tax that renders essential hygiene products unaffordable for the most vulnerable. Our proposal creates a clear, practical roadmap for removing financial barriers and investing in the future of women and girls.

Policy idea: Zero-rate VAT on menstrual products and support local production with duty-free raw materials.

The Impact of Inaction

The High Cost of Silence

Educational Stagnation

Girls are forced to miss class, fall behind on lessons, and risk dropping out because periods are still treated as a private burden rather than a public policy issue.

Public Health Risks

When affordable hygiene products are unavailable, girls resort to unsafe alternatives, increasing the risk of infection and reducing dignity, confidence, and safety.

Economic Exclusion

Women lose productivity in education and work when access to sanitary products is inconsistent, reducing household income and national growth.

Structural Inequality

Taxation as a Burden

Menstrual products in Ethiopia remain treated as non-essential goods rather than health necessities. The current tax burden keeps prices high for households already stretched to the limit.

The hidden barrier: Local manufacturers still face high duties on raw materials, which keeps Ethiopian-made products uncompetitive and stifles job creation.

The Pathway Forward

A Three-Pillar Solution

01
Finance

Zero-Rated VAT

Immediately remove the VAT on sanitary products to reduce retail costs and improve access for lower-income households.

02
Industry

Local Incentives

Exempt imported raw materials for local manufacturers and create public incentives for making essentials affordable at scale.

03
Public Health

Public Provision

Fund national distribution in schools, clinics, prisons, and displacement camps so dignity is not determined by income.
