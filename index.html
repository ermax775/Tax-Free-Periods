Executive Summary

The Crisis of Access

In Ethiopia, menstruation is more than a biological process—it is a barrier to opportunity. Over 70% of women and girls lack the means to manage their periods with dignity. This crisis is fueled by a 'Period Tax' that renders essential hygiene products unaffordable for the most vulnerable.

Our policy proposal outlines a clear, sustainable pathway to eliminate these financial barriers and unlock the potential of millions. By removing the fiscal burden on health essentials, we invest in the foundation of our nation's future: the education and health of our girls.

A Case for Change

Amina’s Story: A Future on Hold

"Every month I feel like I am losing my future because I cannot afford a packet of pads."

Amina is a 14-year-old student in rural Amhara with dreams of becoming a doctor. But every month, she faces a choice no child should make: attend school without protection or stay home in shame.

Amina misses 50 days of school a year—a direct result of period poverty. This is not just a personal struggle; it is a systemic failure to provide the basic infrastructure for a girl to succeed.

Structural Inequality

Taxation as a Burden

Currently, menstrual products in Ethiopia are subject to a 15% Value Added Tax (VAT), categorizing them as luxury goods rather than essential health items.

The Hidden Barrier

While some finished product duties have been reduced, the raw materials required for local production face customs duties as high as 35%.

This effectively penalizes Ethiopian manufacturers and keeps prices artificially high, stifling the 'Made in Ethiopia' economy while keeping girls out of the classroom.

The Pathway Forward

A Three-Pillar Solution

01

Zero-Rated VAT

Immediately remove the 15% VAT on all menstrual hygiene products to lower the retail price for consumers.

Ministry of Finance
02

Local Incentives

Grant duty-free status to raw material imports for local manufacturers to boost the 'Made in Ethiopia' economy.

Ministry of Industry
03

Public Provision

Establish a national budget for the free distribution of products in schools, prisons, and displacement camps.

Education & Social Affairs

Implementation Feasibility

The transition to a tax-free period model is both achievable and economically sound. By phasing in VAT exemptions over 12 months, the government can monitor market price adjustments.

The short-term tax revenue loss is significantly outweighed by the long-term economic gains from increased female education and workforce participation. This is not a cost; it is an investment in human capital.
